why this week exists

Most days run on autopilot.

Nothing is wrong with you. An ordinary day simply carries more than attention can hold, so life starts happening on its own. The way back is smaller than you think: one pause, felt all the way through.

Most days, we are somewhere else. This week, we look up together.

Your host: Cory Allen

meditation teacher · author · the astral hustle

You may already know his voice. Across ten years and hundreds of conversations on The Astral Hustle, Cory has asked one question from every angle: how do you actually stay present in a loud life. His answer starts where this week starts. The problem is volume, and volume can be turned down.

He opens the week in person, holds the theme each morning, and leads a live call every day. Each call is a real conversation, and you are in it.

the shape of the week

Four moments, one circle.

The week has a beginning you can feel, a rhythm you can keep, and an ending that closes what it opened.

01 · the shared moment
You take your seat.

One room in Austin, every living room on the call, and the week is suddenly real. Everyone arrives at once.

02 · the kickoff
Cory sets the theme.

He opens the week live: why this theme, why now, and the one small practice you carry into the first morning.

03 · the practice
A pause, every day.

A few quiet minutes each morning, a live call each day, and a small room of people noticing out loud together.

04 · full circle
The circle closes.

On the last evening you return to where you began, say what you noticed, and choose what comes with you.

the practice moment

A few minutes that give the day back.

Each morning, a short prompt from Cory lands where your friends already talk. One theme all week, so the practice gets deeper instead of wider.

Each day, a live 30-minute call to say what you noticed and hear what everyone else did. You may find that's the part you look forward to.
